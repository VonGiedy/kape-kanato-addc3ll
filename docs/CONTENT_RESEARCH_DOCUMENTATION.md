## Module 1: Coffee Fundamentals & Botany

### Topic 1: What is Coffee? The Anatomy of a Cherry
**Assigned section:** Coffee Fundamentals (Module 1)  
**Topic title:** What Is Coffee? The Anatomy of a Cherry  
**Introduction:**  
Coffee is far more than a morning beverage; it is the processed and roasted seed of a tropical fruit tree. Understanding coffee starts at the botanical level by examining the physiological layers of the coffee cherry and how each layer impacts fermentation, drying, and eventual cup quality.  

**Main content:**  
Every cup of coffee begins its life on an evergreen shrub belonging to the genus *Coffea*. While colloquially referred to as "beans," they are not legumes—they are the dual seeds nestled at the core of a small, bright red, orange, or yellow fruit known as a coffee cherry.

The anatomical structure of a coffee cherry consists of six distinct concentric layers:
1. **Exocarp (Outer Skin):** The tough, taut outer skin that transitions from deep green to bright crimson, orange, or yellow as chloroplasts break down and anthocyanins develop during ripening.
2. **Mesocarp (Pulp):** A fleshy fruit layer rich in simple sugars, water, and organic acids that nourishes the seeds inside.
3. **Pectin Layer (Mucilage):** A dense, sticky, gelatinous layer composed of polysaccharides (pectin, cellulose), sugars, and water. This layer is critical during fermentation, serving as the biological fuel for natural yeasts and lactic acid bacteria.
4. **Endocarp (Parchment / Pergamino):** A stiff, fibrous cellulose hull that encases each seed, shielding it from external pathogens and physical damage during resting and aging (*reposo*).
5. **Spermoderm (Silverskin):** A micro-thin, glossy membrane adhering tightly to the green seed. During roasting, this layer desiccates and detaches as "chaff."
6. **Endosperm (The Seed / Bean):** The true coffee bean—an embryo and nutrient reservoir packed with proteins, chlorogenic acids, sucrose, caffeine, and aromatic precursors that chemically react during roasting.

In approximately 5% of cherries, a genetic variation or pollination phenomenon occurs where only one seed develops inside the fruit, taking on a rounded, football-like shape. This is called a **Peaberry** (*Caracol*). Peaberries roll smoothly inside roasting drums and conduct convective heat uniformly.  

**Key takeaways:**  
* Coffee beans are botanical seeds of a tropical fruit cherry, not true legumes.
* The sugar-rich mucilage layer dictates the fermentation kinetics in natural and honey processing.
* Peaberries occur naturally in roughly 5% of cherries and feature unique spherical density and heat conduction properties.

### Topic 2: The Four Commercial Coffee Species
**Assigned section:** Coffee Fundamentals (Module 1)  
**Topic title:** The Four Commercial Coffee Species (Arabica, Robusta, Liberica, Excelsa)  
**Introduction:**  
While *Coffea arabica* and *Coffea canephora* (Robusta) dominate over 98% of international global commerce, the Philippines is one of the rare countries worldwide with commercial production and favorable agro-climatic conditions for all four primary coffee species: Arabica, Robusta, Liberica, and Excelsa.  

**Main content:**  
Over 120 species of the genus *Coffea* exist in the wild, but four species form the backbone of commercial agriculture:

1. **Coffea Arabica (Specialty & High-Altitude):**
   * *Genetics & Altitude:* Tetraploid (44 chromosomes), self-pollinating. Thrives in cool highland climates (1,000 to 2,200 MASL).
   * *Chemical Profile:* Contains ~1.2% caffeine, high lipid content (15–17%), and high sucrose (6–9%).
   * *Sensory Attributes:* Delicate floral notes (jasmine, coffee blossom), sparkling fruit acidity (citric, malic), complex sweetness, and tea-like to medium body.
   * *Mindanao Hotspots:* Mount Apo (Davao del Sur, Cotabato) and Mount Kitanglad/Kalatungan (Bukidnon).

2. **Coffea Canephora (Robusta):**
   * *Genetics & Altitude:* Diploid (22 chromosomes), cross-pollinated. Highly resilient lowland crop (200 to 1,000 MASL).
   * *Chemical Profile:* High caffeine content (~2.2% to 2.7%), high chlorogenic acid, lower lipids and sucrose.
   * *Sensory Attributes:* Full-bodied, heavy crema production, notes of dark chocolate, roasted nuts, grain, and spice. Through ADDC3LL's "Fine Robusta" processing protocols, Robusta develops sweet, clean, fruit-forward profiles.
   * *Mindanao Hotspots:* Sultan Kudarat, Davao Region, Basilan, Sulu.

3. **Coffea Liberica (Kapeng Barako):**
   * *Genetics & Altitude:* Native to West Africa. Towering trees growing up to 15–20 meters with massive leathery leaves and giant, asymmetrical cherries.
   * *Chemical Profile:* Low to moderate caffeine (~1.2–1.5%), high sugar, unique volatile aromatics.
   * *Sensory Attributes:* Explosive, pungent aroma reminiscent of ripe jackfruit (*langka*), anise, dark cacao, and woody cedar.
   * *Hotspots:* Batangas, Cavite, and emerging agroforestry farms in Mindanao.

4. **Coffea Excelsa (Liberica var. dewevrei):**
   * *Genetics & Altitude:* Historically classified as a standalone species, now classified taxonomically as a variety of Liberica. Represents roughly 7% of Philippine coffee.
   * *Sensory Attributes:* Tart, dark, and fruity cup profile with lingering blackberry, dark date, and balsamic undertones. Often blended to add depth, fruit complexity, and body.
   * *Hotspots:* Sultan Kudarat, Cavite, and Bukidnon.

**Key takeaways:**  
* The Philippines actively cultivates all four commercial species: Arabica, Robusta, Liberica, and Excelsa.
* Arabica provides nuanced acidity and aromatics; Robusta contributes dense body, high crema, and caffeine; Liberica delivers bold jackfruit and cacao intensity.
* Mindanao's Fine Robusta movement is dispelling the myth that Robusta is strictly an inferior commodity bean.

### Topic 3: History of Coffee: From Ancient Ethiopia to the Philippine Resurgence
**Assigned section:** Coffee Fundamentals (Module 1)  
**Topic title:** History of Coffee: From Ancient Ethiopia to the Philippine Resurgence  
**Introduction:**  
Tracing coffee's evolutionary and cultural trajectory reveals how a wild Ethiopian understory shrub catalyzed global trade, spurred European intellectual Enlightenment coffeehouses, established an economic boom in 19th-century Batangas, and transformed Mindanao into the country's specialty coffee heartland today.  

**Main content:**  
* **The Ethiopian Origins (circa 850 AD):**  
  Oral legend credits Kaldi, an Ethiopian goat herder in Kaffa, who noticed his goats gamboling energetically after consuming red berries from a wild bush. Nearby Sufi monks boiled the cherries into an invigorating broth (*qahwah*) that sustained nighttime vigils and prayer.
* **The Arabian Monopoly and Coffeehouses (15th–17th Century):**  
  Coffee cultivation expanded across Yemen through the port of Mocha (*Al-Mukha*). The Ottoman Empire codified coffee preparation, opening *qahveh khaneh* (coffee houses) that became hubs for political discourse, poetry, and chess, later spreading to Venice, London, and Paris.
* **Arrival in the Philippines (1740):**  
  In 1740, a Spanish Franciscan friar introduced the first coffee seeds to Lipa, Batangas. The fertile volcanic loam of Mount Malarayat proved ideal. By the 1880s, when Coffee Leaf Rust (*Hemileia vastatrix*) devastated plantations in Ceylon (Sri Lanka) and Java (Indonesia), the Philippines became the 4th largest coffee exporter in the world. Lipa was hailed as the "Coffee Capital of the World."
* **The Leaf Rust Collapse & Species Shift:**  
  In 1889, coffee leaf rust arrived in Batangas, destroying vast Arabica estates within years. Filipino growers responded by transitioning to rust-resilient Liberica (*Barako*) and Robusta, while Arabica found cool, high-altitude sanctuary on the volcanic slopes of Mindanao and the Cordilleras.
* **The Mindanao Specialty Resurgence (Present Day):**  
  Today, Mindanao yields 70% to 80% of total national coffee output. Collaborations between academic laboratories like ADDC3LL, farmer cooperatives (BACOFA, MILALITTRA), and industry networks (Mindanao Coffee Network) have spearheaded third-wave specialty and Fine Robusta production.

**Key takeaways:**  
* Coffee originated wild in Ethiopia and was first commercially cultivated and brewed in Yemen.
* In the 1880s, the Philippines surged to the 4th largest coffee exporter globally due to the Lipa boom.
* Today, Mindanao produces 70%–80% of Philippine coffee and leads national specialty quality advancements.

## Module 2: Farm to Cup Journey & Post-Harvest Science

### Topic 4: Post-Harvest Processing: Washed, Natural, Honey & Anaerobic Fermentation
**Assigned section:** Farm to Cup (Module 2)  
**Topic title:** Post-Harvest Processing: Washed, Natural, Honey & Anaerobic Fermentation  
**Introduction:**  
From the second a ripe cherry is harvested, biochemical processes begin. Post-harvest processing describes how coffee seeds are extracted from the fruit pulp and dried down to an export-stable moisture content of 10% to 12%. The chosen processing method profoundly shapes cup profile, acidity, body, and aroma.  

**Main content:**  
The four primary post-harvest methods practiced and researched at ADDC3LL are:

1. **Washed / Wet Processing (Clarity & Vibrant Acidity):**
   * *Process:* Cherries are floated in density tanks to remove underripe floaters, mechanically depulped to remove the exocarp, and fermented in water tanks for 12 to 36 hours. Endogenous enzymes and microbes break down the pectin-rich mucilage layer. Seeds are washed with clean water and sun-dried on raised African beds.
   * *Sensory Profile:* Sparkling cup clarity, prominent citric and malic acidity, light to medium body, distinct terroir expression.
2. **Natural / Dry Processing (Jammy Sweetness & Heavy Body):**
   * *Process:* Whole, intact cherries are sorted and immediately spread onto raised ventilated drying beds for 20 to 30 days. The cherries are turned hourly to prevent mold formation. As the fruit pulp slowly desiccates in the sun, natural sugars and esters migrate inward toward the seed.
   * *Sensory Profile:* Intense berry notes (strawberry, blueberry, jackfruit), syrupy mouthfeel, winey sweetness, lower perceived acidity.
3. **Honey / Pulped Natural Processing (Balanced Mouthfeel):**
   * *Process:* Cherries are depulped, but controlled amounts of the sticky mucilage ("honey") remain coating the parchment during drying. Sub-classified by mucilage retention:
     * *Yellow Honey:* ~25% mucilage, dried quickly in direct sun.
     * *Red Honey:* ~50% mucilage, dried in moderate shade.
     * *Black Honey:* ~100% mucilage, dried in dense shade with slow moisture loss.
   * *Sensory Profile:* Stone fruit notes (apricot, peach), balanced acidity, honeyed sweetness, creamy mouthfeel.
4. **Anaerobic Fermentation & Carbonic Maceration (Cutting-Edge Innovation):**
   * *Process:* Cherries or depulped parchment are sealed inside airtight stainless steel or food-grade polyethylene barrels fitted with one-way airlocks. Deprived of oxygen, anaerobic microorganisms (especially lactic acid bacteria and specific yeasts) flourish, lowering pH and metabolizing complex sugars into volatile esters.
   * *Sensory Profile:* Exotic, complex flavors: tropical fruit punch, cinnamon spice, cacao nibs, lactic creaminess, and sparkling effervescence.

**Key takeaways:**  
* Washed processing showcases bean density and regional acidity without fruit mask.
* Natural processing imparts fruit esters and heavy body directly from desiccating cherry flesh.
* Anaerobic fermentation utilizes controlled microbial kinetics in oxygen-deprived tanks to create exotic, high-scoring competition cup profiles.

### Topic 5: The Science of Coffee Roasting: Thermodynamic Reactions & Heat Transfer
**Assigned section:** Farm to Cup (Module 2)  
**Topic title:** The Science of Coffee Roasting: Thermodynamic Reactions & Heat Transfer  
**Introduction:**  
Green coffee seeds are biologically inert, hard, and smell faintly of dried grass. Only through the controlled application of thermodynamic heat do complex non-enzymatic browning reactions occur, unlocking over 800 volatile organic compounds that create the aroma and flavor of brewed coffee.  

**Main content:**  
Roasting relies on conductive, convective, and radiant heat transfer through four distinct thermal phases:

1. **Drying / Yellowing Phase (Ambient to ~160°C):**
   * *Thermodynamics:* Endothermic reaction. Heat evaporates free water within green seeds (reducing moisture from ~11% to ~2%).
   * *Visual & Aromatic Changes:* Seeds transition from blue-green to golden yellow, emitting aromas of toasted grain, green tea, and warm bread.
2. **Maillard Reaction & Caramelization (160°C to ~200°C):**
   * *Chemistry:* Reducing sugars react with free amino acids to generate melanoidins, furans, and pyrazines. As temperatures exceed 170°C, sucrose caramelizes, producing sweet, buttery, nutty, and chocolate aroma precursors while generating CO2.
   * *Impact:* Builds the coffee's perceived body, sweetness, and tactile mouthfeel.
3. **First Crack (~196°C to 205°C):**
   * *Physics:* Trapped moisture vaporizes into steam, creating internal pressures exceeding 25 atmospheres. When cell walls fracture, an audible popping sound occurs ("First Crack").
   * *Thermodynamic Shift:* The reaction briefly turns exothermic. Marks the birth of Light Roast (City roast). Acidity and origin-specific floral and fruit notes peak here.
4. **Development Phase & Second Crack (>215°C):**
   * *Roast Profiling:* Roasters monitor the Development Time Ratio (DTR, usually 15%–22% of total roast time).
   * *Second Crack (~225°C):* The woody cellular matrix ruptures structurally, releasing CO2 and forcing aromatic bean oils (*caffeol*) to migrate onto the bean surface. Characteristic of Dark Roasts (Vienna, French Roast), where smoky, bittersweet, and roasty notes eclipse origin terroir.

**Key takeaways:**  
* The Maillard reaction synthesizes melanoidins that produce sweetness, aroma, and mouthfeel.
* First Crack occurs as steam pressure ruptures the bean cell walls, signaling light roast maturity.
* Light roasts preserve delicate floral and origin acidity; dark roasts emphasize caramelized sugar, smoke, and surface oils.

## Module 3: Brewing Science & Extraction Theory

### Topic 6: Extraction Theory: The Golden Cup & Dissolution Dynamics
**Assigned section:** Brewing (Module 3)  
**Topic title:** Extraction Theory: The Golden Cup & Dissolution Dynamics  
**Introduction:**  
Brewing coffee is the physical and chemical dissolution of soluble organic compounds from ground coffee cells into hot water. Mastering extraction theory enables students, baristas, and coffee enthusiasts to systematically eliminate sourness or bitterness by manipulating four physical brewing variables.  

**Main content:**  
* **Solubility Limits & The SCA Golden Cup Standard:**  
  Approximately 28% to 30% of a coffee bean's physical mass is water-soluble; the remaining 70% is insoluble cellulose and woody fiber. The SCA Golden Cup Standard defines the optimal **Extraction Yield** as **18% to 22%**, yielding a Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) concentration between **1.15% and 1.45%**.
* **The 3 Sequential Extraction Phases:**  
  Chemical compounds dissolve in hot water at different speeds due to molecular weight and polarity:
  1. *Phase 1 (Fats & Organic Fruit Acids):* Rapid dissolution. Releases bright citric, malic, and phosphoric acids, and delicate volatile floral aromatics.
  2. *Phase 2 (Carbohydrates & Simple Sugars):* Mid-rate dissolution. Releases sucrose, fructose, and lipids that provide sweetness, caramel notes, and velvety mouthfeel.
  3. *Phase 3 (Heavy Polyphenols & Plant Fibers):* Slow dissolution. Insoluble tannins, cellulose, and chlorogenic quinones extract last, contributing bitterness, astringency, and dryness.
* **Diagnosing Cup Flaws:**  
  * *Under-Extraction (<18% Yield):* Brew stopped too early. Lacks sweetness to balance acidity. Tastes sharp, sour, salty, thin, with a sudden finish.
  * *Over-Extraction (>22% Yield):* Brew ran too long or fine. Pulled out bitter fibers and tannins. Tastes dry, chalky, astringent, bitter, resembling medicinal ash or walnut skin.
* **The Four Master Variables:**  
  1. *Grind Size:* Dictates exposed surface area. Fine grinds speed dissolution; coarse grinds slow it down.
  2. *Water Temperature:* Optimal 90°C–96°C. Hotter water accelerates extraction; water below 88°C under-extracts fruit sugars.
  3. *Brew Ratio:* Proportional balance of dry coffee dose to water weight (e.g., 1:15 to 1:17 for filter).
  4. *Contact Time & Agitation:* Total duration of water-ground contact and turbulence.

**Key takeaways:**  
* Optimal extraction yield is 18%–22%; below 18% is sour/under-extracted, above 22% is bitter/dry.
* Organic fruit acids dissolve first, followed by sweet sugars, followed finally by bitter polyphenols.
* Adjust grind size as the primary tool to dial in cup sweetness.

### Topic 7: Precision Brewing Manuals: Step-by-Step Parameter Database
**Assigned section:** Brewing (Module 3)  
**Topic title:** Precision Brewing Manuals: Step-by-Step Parameter Database  
**Introduction:**  
Different manual brewing methods utilize distinct extraction mechanics: percolation (gravity filter), full immersion (steep), pressure infusion, or thermal boiling. This topic catalogs calibrated brewing parameters for the six most popular brewing methods in the Philippines.  

**Main content:**  
1. **Hario V60 (Percolation / Drip):**
   * *Mechanism:* Conical 60° angle with spiral internal ribs promoting fast water flow through paper filtration.
   * *Parameters:* 15g dose / 240g water (Ratio 1:16). Grind: Medium-Fine. Temp: 92°C–94°C. Time: 3:15.
   * *Step Sequence:* 45g Bloom (45s) -> First spiral pour to 140g -> Second pour to 240g -> Drawdown.
   * *Sensory Highlight:* Maximum clarity, bright floral notes, sparkling acidity.
2. **French Press / Cafetière (Full Immersion):**
   * *Mechanism:* Complete submersion in hot water with metal mesh filtration retaining natural coffee oils.
   * *Parameters:* 20g dose / 300g water (Ratio 1:15). Grind: Coarse. Temp: 94°C–96°C. Time: 4:00.
   * *Step Sequence:* Saturate grounds (45s) -> Steep under lid (135s) -> Break crust & skim foam (30s) -> Slow plunge.
   * *Sensory Highlight:* Rich, velvety body, lingering dark chocolate mouthfeel.
3. **AeroPress (Inverted Pressure Infusion):**
   * *Mechanism:* Rapid full immersion combined with pneumatic piston air pressure.
   * *Parameters:* 14g dose / 200g water (Ratio 1:14). Grind: Medium-Fine. Temp: 85°C–88°C. Time: 2:00.
   * *Step Sequence:* Inverted setup & pour -> 3 back-and-forth stirs -> Cap & steep to 1:30 -> Flip & press for 30s.
   * *Sensory Highlight:* High sweetness, clean body, portable versatility.
4. **Cold Brew Steep (Slow Cold Dissolution):**
   * *Mechanism:* Extended immersion in ambient/refrigerated water (14–18 hours) avoiding thermal acid extraction.
   * *Parameters:* 60g dose / 600g water (Ratio 1:10). Grind: Extra Coarse. Temp: 18°C–22°C. Time: 16 hours.
   * *Sensory Highlight:* Ultra-low perceived acidity, deep cocoa and brown sugar sweetness.
5. **Moka Pot / Stovetop Espresso (Vapor Pressure):**
   * *Mechanism:* Steam pressure (1.5 to 2 bars) forces boiling water upward from the lower reservoir through finely ground coffee.
   * *Parameters:* 18g dose / 180g water (Ratio 1:10). Grind: Fine. Temp: Pre-heated 95°C water. Time: 2:30.
   * *Sensory Highlight:* Dense, syrup-like body, sharp intensity, excellent milk base.
6. **Kape Kaldero (Philippine Traditional Pot Brew):**
   * *Mechanism:* Heritage boiling method traditionally used for Kapeng Barako and Mindanao Robusta.
   * *Parameters:* 25g dose / 300g water (Ratio 1:12). Grind: Medium-Coarse. Temp: 95°C. Time: 3:00.
   * *Step Sequence:* Boil water -> Add grounds & optional raw muscovado/panutsa -> Cover & steep 3 min -> Strain through cloth *katsa*.
   * *Sensory Highlight:* Smoky sweet aroma, ripe jackfruit essence, authentic local heritage.

**Key takeaways:**  
* Percolation (V60) emphasizes clarity; immersion (French Press) emphasizes oils and body; pressure (AeroPress/Moka Pot) increases extraction intensity.
* Kape Kaldero is an authentic Philippine brewing tradition optimized for high-body Liberica and Robusta beans.
* Pre-heating Moka Pot lower chamber water avoids scorched, metallic tastes.

### Topic 8: Espresso & Specialty Milk Beverages Liquid Anatomy
**Assigned section:** Brewing (Module 3)  
**Topic title:** Espresso & Specialty Milk Beverages Liquid Anatomy  
**Introduction:**  
Espresso is the foundation of modern specialty coffee retail. By forcing hot water (90°C–96°C) at 9 bars of pressure through a compacted cake of finely ground coffee in 25–30 seconds, a concentrated emulsion of oils, colloids, and micro-bubbles is formed. This topic details the anatomical proportions of eight core espresso drinks.  

**Main content:**  
* **Espresso Extraction Mechanics:**  
  A standard single shot extracts 25–30 ml (approx. 15–18g dry dose into 30–36g liquid out, ratio 1:2) yielding two visual phases: the dark liquid body rich in solubles and the golden-hazelnut **crema** (CO2 gas trapped in liquid surfactant oils).
* **Volumetric Liquid Proportions:**
  1. *Single Espresso:* 1 oz (30 ml) concentrated shot. 80% espresso body, 20% crema. (~63 mg caffeine).
  2. *Espresso Macchiato:* 1.5 oz (45 ml). 75% espresso, topped with a 25% dollop of velvety steamed microfoam.
  3. *Cortado:* 4 oz (120 ml). 50% double espresso, 35% warm steamed milk, 15% silky microfoam. Cuts acidity 1:1.
  4. *Flat White:* 5 oz (150 ml). 40% double ristretto (short, sweet extraction), 45% steamed whole milk, 15% glossy microfoam.
  5. *Traditional Cappuccino:* 6 oz (180 ml). Classic rule of thirds: 1/3 espresso (34%), 1/3 steamed milk (33%), 1/3 aerated milk foam (33%).
  6. *Caffè Latte:* 8–12 oz (240–360 ml). 20% espresso, 65% steamed milk, 15% velvety microfoam canvas for latte art.
  7. *Caffè Americano:* 8–10 oz (240–300 ml). 90% hot water with 10% double espresso poured on top to preserve the sweet crema ring.
  8. *Caffè Mocha (Davao Tablea Infused):* 8 oz (240 ml). 20% espresso shot, 20% melted single-origin Davao dark cacao tablea, 45% steamed milk, 15% microfoam.

**Key takeaways:**  
* Milk texture temperature must remain below 65°C to prevent whey protein denaturing and lactose sweetness degradation.
* The Flat White utilizes double ristretto for a sweeter, less bitter coffee-to-milk intensity than a latte.
* The Tablea Mocha celebrates Mindanao's dual strength in specialty coffee and award-winning single-estate cacao.

### Topic 22: Coffee IQ Assessment: 10 Core Competency Questions & Explanations
**Assigned section:** Educational Assessment & Interactive Tools (Module 7)  
**Topic title:** Coffee IQ Assessment: 10 Core Competency Questions & Explanations  
**Introduction:**  
The Kape Kanato educational web prototype integrates an interactive 10-question Coffee IQ quiz designed to test student comprehension across agronomy, processing chemistry, roasting thermodynamics, brewing science, and Philippine heritage.  

**Main content:**  
* **Quiz Question Bank & Calibrated Explanations:**
  1. *Q: Which Philippine island produces roughly 70% to 80% of the country's total coffee output?*  
     *Answer:* Mindanao.  
     *Explanation:* Mindanao is the coffee heartland of the Philippines, producing the vast majority of commercial and specialty crops across Mt. Apo, Bukidnon, and Sultan Kudarat.
  2. *Q: What is Kapeng Barako botanically classified as?*  
     *Answer:* Coffea liberica.  
     *Explanation:* Kapeng Barako is Coffea liberica, characterized by towering tree heights, giant asymmetric seeds, and pungent floral-jackfruit aromatics.
  3. *Q: During coffee extraction, which compounds dissolve into hot water first?*  
     *Answer:* Fats, organic fruit acids, and floral aromatics.  
     *Explanation:* Fast-dissolving organic acids extract first, followed by sweet carbohydrates, and lastly by slow-extracting bitter polyphenols and fibers.
  4. *Q: What audible event in coffee roasting marks the beginning of Light Roast development?*  
     *Answer:* First Crack.  
     *Explanation:* First Crack occurs around 196°C–205°C as steam pressure fractures bean cell walls, signaling the onset of light roast development.
  5. *Q: What is a "Peaberry"?*  
     *Answer:* A naturally occurring single round seed inside a cherry.  
     *Explanation:* In ~5% of cherries, only a single rounded seed forms instead of twin beans, prized for uniform heat conduction.
  6. *Q: Which post-harvest process involves fermenting whole, intact cherries under the sun for 20–30 days?*  
     *Answer:* Natural / Dry process.  
     *Explanation:* Natural processing dries whole cherries on raised beds, allowing fruit sugars and esters to infuse the seed with heavy sweetness and berry notes.
  7. *Q: What is the target Extraction Yield range for balanced coffee brewing per SCA standards?*  
     *Answer:* 18% to 22%.  
     *Explanation:* The sweet spot where sugars, lipids, and fruit acids balance without harsh bitterness (>22%) or sour sharpness (<18%).
  8. *Q: In which decade did the Philippines surge to become the 4th largest coffee exporter in the world?*  
     *Answer:* 1880s (The Lipa, Batangas boom).  
     *Explanation:* When leaf rust decimated Ceylon and Java, the Philippines briefly surged to 4th largest global exporter before leaf rust struck in 1889.
  9. *Q: What is the approximate elevation range of specialty Arabica grown on Mount Apo?*  
     *Answer:* 1,200 to 1,850+ MASL.  
     *Explanation:* High volcanic elevations provide cool night temperatures that slow fruit maturation, creating dense beans with high sugar content.
  10. *Q: Which Ateneo de Davao facility in Marilog received a 10-year registered trademark for roasting & processing in 2026?*  
      *Answer:* ADDC3LL.  
      *Explanation:* ADDC3LL secured registered trademark status under Class 40 with IPOPHL on February 27, 2026, valid until 2036.

* 10 calibrated questions assessing core coffee competencies from farm agronomy to brewing physics.

### Topic 23: Comprehensive A–Z Coffee & Cacao Glossary Reference
**Assigned section:** Educational Assessment & Interactive Tools (Module 7)  
**Topic title:** Comprehensive A–Z Coffee & Cacao Glossary Reference  
**Introduction:**  
To support academic literacy for students, baristas, and researchers, Kape Kanato maintains a searchable A–Z glossary of 25+ essential coffee, agronomy, sensory, and institutional terms.  

**Main content:**  
* **Glossary Terms & Official Definitions:**
  * **Acidity (Sensory):** The pleasant, sparkling sensation on the tongue that gives coffee liveliness and fruit character (citric, malic, phosphoric). Not sourness.
  * **ADDC3LL (Institution):** Ateneo de Davao Coffee and Cacao Collaborative Learning Laboratory. A university initiative based in Marilog, Davao City in partnership with the Mindanao Coffee Network, dedicated to research, technology transfer, and agro-enterprise development, with an IPOPHL Class 40 trademark.
  * **Aerobic Fermentation (Processing):** Fermentation occurring in the presence of oxygen where microbes digest the mucilage surrounding the parchment.
  * **Anaerobic Fermentation (Processing):** Controlled fermentation in oxygen-deprived tanks fitted with one-way release valves, yielding distinct lactic and tropical esters.
  * **Arabica / *Coffea arabica* (Botany):** A high-altitude species containing 44 chromosomes. Yields complex floral and sweetly acidic flavors with lower caffeine than Robusta.
  * **Barako (Heritage):** The Filipino vernacular for *Coffea liberica*, named after the wild boar. Famed for giant trees, large seeds, and intense floral-jackfruit notes.
  * **Bloom (Brewing):** The rapid degassing of CO2 when hot water hits fresh coffee grounds, causing the coffee bed to swell.
  * **Body / Mouthfeel (Sensory):** The tactile perception of weight and thickness on the palate, ranging from light and tea-like to creamy or syrupy.
  * **Brix (°Bx) (Agronomy):** A refractometric measurement of dissolved sugar content in coffee cherry fruit sap (typically 18°–24° Brix at peak ripeness).
  * **Chaff (Roasting):** The papery silverskin (*spermoderm*) that separates and flakes off during bean expansion in roasting.
  * **Channeling (Brewing):** An extraction defect where water finds narrow paths of least resistance through the coffee bed, causing localized over-extraction.
  * **Crema (Espresso):** The golden-reddish emulsion of aromatic oils and trapped CO2 gas floating on top of an espresso shot.
  * **Cupping (Sensory):** The standardized global protocol established by the SCA for evaluating the fragrance, aroma, flavor, acidity, body, and balance of coffee.
  * **Degassing (Roasting):** The natural discharge of CO2 gas from roasted coffee beans over 3–14 days following roasting.
  * **Excelsa / *Coffea excelsa* (Botany):** A variety of *Coffea liberica* contributing tart, dark fruit, and blackberry complexity.
  * **Extraction Yield (Brewing):** The percentage of dry coffee mass dissolved into liquid brew, targeting 18% to 22% for balanced flavor.
  * **First Crack (Roasting):** The audible popping sound around 196°C–205°C signaling the fracturing of bean cell walls and the onset of light roast.
  * **Kahawa Sug (Heritage):** The centuries-old cultural tradition of black, strong coffee drinking among the Tausug people of Sulu.
  * **Liberica / *Coffea liberica* (Botany):** Towering coffee tree species known for asymmetric beans and jackfruit-anise aromatics.
  * **Maillard Reaction (Roasting):** The chemical reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars synthesizing melanoidins and flavor precursors.
  * **MASL (Agronomy):** Meters Above Sea Level. Elevation metric directly influencing bean density and maturation speed.
  * **Mucilage (Botany):** The gelatinous, sugar-rich pectin layer between the coffee cherry pulp and the parchment hull.
  * **Peaberry (Botany):** A single round seed occurring in ~5% of cherries when only one ovule is fertilized.
  * **Robusta / *Coffea canephora* (Botany):** Hardy, disease-resistant lowland species with high caffeine (~2.2%) and heavy body.
  * **Second Crack (Roasting):** The structural rupture of the bean cellulose matrix around 225°C, characteristic of dark roasts.
  * **TDS (Brewing):** Total Dissolved Solids. Percentage of dissolved coffee solids in a cup, measured via digital refractometer.

**Key takeaways:**  
* Provides standard definitions for technical terms across agronomy, botany, processing, roasting, brewing, and heritage.
* Encodes official institutional definitions for ADDC3LL and regional cultural landmarks like Kahawa Sug.
* Accessible via global search modal and dedicated A–Z reference drawer.
